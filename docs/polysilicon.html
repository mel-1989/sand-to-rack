Siemens CVD reactor

Sand to polysilicon

Every chip starts as sand. Quartz is turned into silicon that is about 98.5 % pure, then into a liquid that can be distilled, then back into solid silicon so clean that only about one atom in ten billion is something else. The last step happens in a Siemens reactor, where silicon grows on glowing rods under a steel bell.

What happens

  1. Metallurgical silicon. Quartz and carbon are heated in an electric arc furnace running above 2000 °C. The carbon pulls the oxygen off the silica and leaves silicon that is 98.5–99.5 % pure: fine for aluminium alloys, far too dirty for a transistor.
  2. Hydrochlorination. The silicon is ground and blown with hydrogen chloride through a fluidised bed at about 300 °C. It leaves as trichlorosilane, SiHCl₃, with some silicon tetrachloride beside it (Si + 3.1 HCl → 0.9 SiHCl₃ + 0.1 SiCl₄ + 1.1 H₂).
  3. Distillation. SiHCl₃ boils at 31.8 °C, so the metals, boron and phosphorus that would spoil a chip can be separated from it by their different boiling points: lighter compounds boil off first, and the metal chlorides stay behind in the bottom of the column. Electronic grade runs it through about 5 distillation passes; solar grade about 3.
  4. Deposition. In the reactor, thin silicon rods stand in pairs on graphite chucks, bridged at the top into hairpins. Current through the rods heats them to about 1100 °C. SiHCl₃ and hydrogen flow in through the baseplate and silicon deposits on the hot rods (SiHCl₃ + H₂ → Si + 3 HCl). The rods grow from 20 mm to 150–180 mm across. Fraunhofer ISE puts the growth rate for electronic grade at roughly 1 mm an hour. The double steel wall is water cooled so silicon does not deposit on it.
  5. Harvest. The reactor is cooled, the bell jar is lifted off and the rods are pulled out. They are crushed into chunks, etched in acid to clean the surface, and sent to the crystal pullers of the next chapter.

Why it is built this way

  • Silicon cannot be distilled, but trichlorosilane can. Turning the metal into a low-boiling liquid is what makes purification to parts per trillion possible.
  • The rods are heated by their own current while the bell wall is cooled, so silicon lands only where it is wanted. The price is energy: about 66 kWh for every kilogram in the reactor alone, and producers recover 70–85 % of it as heat to run the distillation columns.
  • Electronic grade grows more slowly than solar grade (roughly 1 mm/h against 1.9, per Fraunhofer ISE) in smaller reactors, because slow, even growth keeps the rod dense and the impurity count low.
  • Hydrogen, HCl and silicon tetrachloride leaving the reactor are recovered and fed back into the earlier steps, so the plant runs as a closed chlorine loop.
