Liquid-cooled compute tray

From package to rack

A finished GPU package is not useful alone. It goes onto a board, under a cold plate, into a 1U tray, and the tray goes into a rack where 72 GPUs share one network, one coolant loop and one power bus. This chapter follows one NVIDIA GB200 NVL72 compute tray from parts on the bench to a running rack.

What happens

  1. Packages onto the boards. Each tray holds 2 GB200 superchips. A superchip is one Grace CPU and two Blackwell GPUs on one board, so the tray carries 4 GPUs and 2 CPUs. Each Blackwell GPU is 2 dies with 208 billion transistors, joined at 10 TB/s.
  2. Cold plates clamp down. Copper cold plates press onto every GPU and CPU, held by spring screws. Hoses link them to a small manifold at the back of the tray. About 115 kW of a rack's heat leaves by liquid; the drives, network cards and risers that remain (about 17 kW) are cooled by fans.
  3. The tray closes. The lid goes on over a 1U chassis (44.45 mm tall). The front holds 4 ConnectX-7 network cards, 2 BlueField-3 DPUs and 4 E1.S drives in NVIDIA's DGX configuration.
  4. It slides into the rack. Pushing the tray home makes three blind connections at once: two UQD04 quick-disconnects onto the rack's coolant manifold, a clip onto the 50–51 V DC bus bar, and the NVLink connectors into the cable cartridge. Nobody plugs a hose or a cable by hand.
  5. Coolant flows. The rack manifold feeds every tray in parallel. The design allows water in at up to 45 °C and back at up to 65 °C, with up to 130 litres a minute for the rack.
  6. The NVLink spine connects. Each GPU has 18 NVLink ports. 4 cartridges of copper cables (over 5,000) run each port to one of 18 NVSwitch chips in the 9 switch trays. 72 × 18 = 1,296 GPU ports meet 9 × 2 × 72 = 1,296 switch ports, so every GPU reaches every other in one hop.

Why it is built this way

  • Copper, not optics, inside the rack. Keeping all 72 GPUs within about a metre lets copper cables carry NVLink without optical transceivers and the power they draw. That short reach is why the rack is packed so densely.
  • Blind-mate liquid cooling. Air cannot move about 120 kW out of one rack. Cold plates and dripless quick-disconnects that mate as the tray slides in make liquid cooling serviceable like an air-cooled server.
  • A rack-wide DC bus bar. Power shelves convert AC to about 50 V DC once, and every tray clips onto the same bar, so there are no power supplies inside the trays. NVIDIA's OCP design doubles the bus bar current to 1,400 A in the same width as ORv3.
  • What comes next. Later racks such as GB300 NVL72 and Vera Rubin NVL72 keep this 72-GPU layout and swap in newer packages; the Rubin package from the previous chapter goes into the same kind of tray. Links that must leave the rack are where optics, and later co-packaged optics, take over.
