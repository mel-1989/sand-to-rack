Wafer-scale and 3D compute

Every other chip in this course is cut out of its wafer. Cerebras keeps the wafer whole: the WSE-3 is one 215 mm square of silicon with 900,000 cores, and the hard part is no longer the chip but the block that feeds it power, carries its heat away and holds it flat. Press Play to build the engine and watch data cross it; the steps below follow the slider.

What happens

  1. Seat the power modules. Power conversion sits under the board, so current can come up through it, perpendicular to the wafer: no copper plane could spread that much current sideways across 215 mm.
  2. Lower the main board. The board carries current and signals up to the wafer through its thickness, with decoupling capacitors close to the connector and I/O connectors at its edge.
  3. Lay the connector on. Silicon and circuit board expand at different rates as they heat, so a custom connector sits between them and absorbs the movement while keeping every contact made.
  4. Set the wafer down. Before this, the stepper printed the wafer one die site per exposure, and Cerebras and TSMC added wires across the scribe lines so its 84 die sites form one array. Spare cores and links are designed in (about 1.5% of the area on the first WSE), so a bad core is switched off and the mesh routes round it. Then it is cut to a 215 mm square, 46,225 mm² or about 65% of the wafer's area; the square's diagonal is 304 mm, so its corners run just off the 300 mm wafer's edge and are clipped.
  5. Clamp the cold plate, turn on power and water. Water carries the heat out through a cold plate pressed on the wafer's back.
  6. Run the mesh. Each core owns 48 kB of SRAM and sends or receives a 32-bit message (a wavelet) to or from a neighbour in a single clock cycle. Data streams from core to core; nothing goes off-chip to fetch weights held on the wafer.

Where wafer-scale and 3D compute go next

  • The same wafer, run faster. The CS-4, shown at Hot Chips 2026, keeps the WSE-3's silicon but runs it as the WSE-3T ("Turbo") at about 2× the clock: 53.5 PB/s of on-wafer fabric bandwidth (Cerebras) and about 43 PB/s of memory bandwidth (ServeTheHome), with 3 wafers in each Nexus rack. The figure draws the WSE-3 and its March 2024 numbers.
  • Power right at the silicon. At Hot Chips 2026 Cerebras said its CS-4 places power conversion about 0.5 mm from the wafer, against about 50 mm on GPU boards, with no circuit board in the last step.
  • System-on-wafer packaging. TSMC's 2024 roadmap for a 2027 full-wafer system (since named SoW-X) promised more than 40 reticles of silicon and room for more than 60 HBM stacks (IEEE Spectrum); TSMC's 2025 release quotes 40× the compute of current CoWoS. It is built from separately tested dies rather than one uncut wafer.
  • Memory stacked on logic. Hybrid bonding joins copper pads directly, with no solder: AMD's 3D V-Cache stacks an SRAM die on a CPU die at a 9 µm pitch, against 50 µm for microbumps. Cerebras plans 3D-stacked DRAM on the wafer for its CS-6.
  • Why bother. Moving data between chips costs far more time and energy than moving it across one piece of silicon. Wafer-scale and 3D stacking both shorten that distance.
