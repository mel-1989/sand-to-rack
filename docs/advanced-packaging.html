2.5D packaging: the Rubin package

A modern AI accelerator is not one chip but a small city of them. NVIDIA's Rubin GPU puts 2 reticle-sized compute dies and 8 stacks of HBM4 memory side by side on a silicon-bridged interposer, all under one cold plate. Pull it apart layer by layer, or press Assemble.

What happens

  1. Substrate. An organic build-up substrate with a ball grid underneath carries power in and NVLink 6 out at 3.6 TB/s. A stiffener ring keeps the large package flat.
  2. Interposer. CoWoS-L embeds small local silicon bridges in a redistribution layer, so only the short, dense die-to-memory and die-to-die wires run in silicon: 16,384 memory data lines in all.
  3. HBM4. Each stack is a base logic die plus 12 DRAM dies joined by through-silicon vias: 36 GB and a 2,048-bit interface per stack.
  4. Compute dies. Two dies at the reticle limit, about the largest area one lithography exposure can print, joined by NV-HBI so software sees one GPU.
  5. Cold plate. JEDEC caps an HBM4 stack at 775 µm. The compute dies are set level with the stacks so one liquid cold plate can press on all ten.

Why it is built this way

  • One die cannot be larger than the reticle (about 26 × 33 mm), so more compute means more dies, and the dies must talk over very short, very wide links.
  • Memory bandwidth, not arithmetic, limits most AI workloads; stacking DRAM right beside the compute dies is what makes terabytes per second possible.
  • Bridges instead of a full silicon interposer let the package grow past the size a single silicon interposer can be made.

How it is assembled

  1. Interposer. Redistribution layers are built on a carrier wafer, with small local silicon interconnect (LSI) bridges embedded where the dies meet the memory.
  2. Chip on wafer. The compute dies and HBM stacks are placed face-down onto the interposer wafer and joined with copper micro-bumps (not hybrid bonds), then underfilled.
  3. Mold and grind. The assembly is molded and ground flat, the carrier is removed, and C4 bumps are formed on the underside.
  4. Chip on substrate. The interposer module is cut out and flip-chip mounted on the organic ABF build-up substrate, underfilled again, and a stiffener ring is fitted.
  5. Test. The finished package is tested before it goes to the board and the cold plate.

Source: TSMC CoWoS overview. The exact Rubin flow is not public; this is the general CoWoS-L sequence.
