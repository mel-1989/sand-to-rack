FOUP, overhead hoist transport and load port

Inside the fab

A wafer never travels alone and never touches open air. It rides with 24 others in a sealed pod called a FOUP, carried by vehicles that run on rails under the cleanroom ceiling, from tool to tool, for about 3 months. Press Play to follow one wafer through one visit to one tool.

What happens

  1. Stocker to rail. Between tools, lots wait in a stocker. An overhead hoist transport (OHT) vehicle picks up the FOUP from the stocker's port and drives along a ceiling rail. A large fab has up to 10 km of rail and several hundred vehicles, and one vehicle covers about 100 km a day.
  2. Hoist down. Above the tool, the vehicle stops and lowers the FOUP on belts onto the load port's kinematic pins, 900 mm above the floor, the height SEMI E15.1 fixes for every tool. An optical hand-off link (SEMI E84) lets vehicle and port agree when it is safe to let go.
  3. Door opens. The load port docks the FOUP against its plate, unlatches the FOUP door, pulls it inside and lowers it, so the wafers face the tool's clean front end without ever meeting the room air.
  4. Robot to tool. Inside the equipment front end module (EFEM), a robot slides a thin fork under one wafer, lifts it from its slot (slots are 10 mm apart), turns and passes it through a slit valve into the tool's load lock.
  5. Process and return. The slit valve shuts while the tool works. Then the robot brings the wafer back to its slot, the door closes, and an OHT lifts the FOUP away to the next tool.
  6. Around and around. A wafer repeats this loop of deposit, pattern, etch, implant, polish and inspect hundreds of times, layer on layer: an advanced logic flow has 600 to 1,000 steps or more, and SemiEngineering put 7 nm at 80 to 85 mask layers.

Why it is built this way

  • Clean where it counts. Sealing the wafers in FOUPs and opening them only inside small filtered minienvironments lets the bay run at ISO 5 while the wafer sees ISO 3 or better.
  • Standards make it a system. Because SEMI fixes the FOUP, the port height and the hand-off signals, a vehicle from one maker can serve a tool from any other, and a fab can mix hundreds of tools from dozens of vendors.
  • 300 mm wafers need machines, not people. A full FOUP weighs 7–9 kg, so automated handling is essential in all but the smallest fabs, and moving it on ceiling rails keeps the floor free for tools.
  • Cycle time is money. Each extra patterning layer adds another lap of the loop, about 1–1.5 days each, so automation that cuts waiting at every tool shortens the months a chip takes to make.
