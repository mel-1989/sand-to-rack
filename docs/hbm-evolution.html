HBM stacks through the generations

Stacked memory: HBM1 to HBM4

An AI accelerator spends much of its time waiting for memory, so the memory sits right beside it as stacks of DRAM dies joined by vertical wires. Each generation of this high-bandwidth memory (HBM) has stacked more dies, run each pin faster and, with HBM4, doubled the number of pins. Press Play to send the height gauge along the bench, one generation at a time.

What happens

  1. HBM (2013). JEDEC's first standard stacks 4 DRAM dies on a base die and gives each stack a 1,024-bit interface at 1 Gb/s a pin: 128 GB/s and 1 GB. AMD's Fury X carried 4 of them in 2015.
  2. HBM2 and HBM2E (2016–2020). Eight-high stacks and faster pins: 2 Gb/s then 3.6 Gb/s, so 256 then 460 GB/s, and 8 then 16 GB a stack as each die grew from 8 to 16 Gb.
  3. HBM3 (2022). The pin rate doubles again to 6.4 Gb/s (819 GB/s a stack), the channels go from 8 to 16, and stacks reach 12 dies. Twelve dies fit in the same 720 µm as eight because each die is ground thinner.
  4. HBM3E (2024). 9.6 Gb/s a pin, about 1.2 TB/s a stack, and 36 GB in a 12-high stack of 24 Gb dies.
  5. HBM4 (2025). The interface doubles to 2,048 bits for the first time since HBM began, so 8 Gb/s a pin gives 2 TB/s a stack. The base die becomes a logic die made in a foundry, and the height limit rises to 775 µm.
  6. Next: HBM4E and HBM5. 16 and then 20 dies are expected. Past about 16, the micro-bumps between dies are expected to give way to hybrid bonding, where copper pads on facing dies fuse directly with no bump and no gap.

Key innovations

  • Through-silicon vias. Copper-filled holes run straight through each thinned die, so every die in the stack reaches the base die over thousands of short vertical wires instead of long bond wires.
  • Thinner dies, same height. Grinding each DRAM die thinner is what lets 8, then 12, then 16 dies fit under one height limit, so a stack sits level with the compute die beside it under one cold plate.
  • Gap fill. SK hynix's mass reflow molded underfill (MR-MUF) floods the gaps between all dies at once; Samsung and Micron laminate a film between dies and bond each one under heat and pressure. Both remove heat and keep thin dies from warping.
  • A logic base die. In HBM4 the bottom die is built on a foundry logic process (SK hynix uses TSMC), with faster, smaller transistors that drive twice as many pins at lower power.
  • Hybrid bonding next. Bonding copper pad to copper pad with no bump removes the gap between dies, which saves height for 16 to 20 dies and shortens the path heat must cross.
