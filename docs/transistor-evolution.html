Planar, FinFET, nanosheet and CFET transistors

The transistor, generation by generation

A transistor is a switch: a voltage on the gate lets current flow from source to drain through the channel below it, or stops it. Every generation since 2007 has changed the shape of that channel so the gate can grip it from more sides, because a gate that only touches one side cannot fully shut a very short channel off. Slide each specimen open to see the cut through its gate.

What happens

  1. Planar MOSFET. The channel is the flat top of the silicon, and the gate sits on it through a thin insulator. Intel's Intel 45 nm process (2007) had a 35 nm gate and a 160 nm contacted gate pitch. Strained silicon from the 90 nm node (2003) sped the carriers up; a high-k dielectric with a metal gate cut the current that leaked straight through an oxide only a few atoms thick.
  2. FinFET. In 2011 Intel stood the channel up as a fin, 8 nm wide and 34 nm tall, so the gate wraps three sides of it. Fins sit 60 nm apart, the contacted gate pitch fell to 90 nm, and density went from 3.3 to 15.3 million transistors per mm².
  3. Gate-all-around nanosheets. The fin is replaced by a stack of thin horizontal sheets with gate material filling the gaps, so the gate surrounds each sheet on four sides. Samsung shipped the first in 2022; TSMC's N2 and Intel 18A (RibbonFET, 50 nm contacted poly pitch) followed in 2025.
  4. CFET. A logic gate needs an n-type and a p-type transistor side by side. A complementary FET stacks the n-type sheets directly on the p-type sheets under one gate, so the pair takes the floor space of one. Intel showed a stacked CFET at a 60 nm gate pitch in 2023; imec's roadmap puts it in production around 2033.

Why the gate had to wrap around

  • Until the mid-2000s, shrinking a transistor also lowered its voltage, so power per area stayed flat (Dennard scaling). That stopped around 2005–2007: leakage current and threshold voltage do not shrink with size, so voltage could no longer fall with each node.
  • With a very short channel, the drain's electric field reaches under the gate and lets current leak even when the gate says off. Every side the gate adds gives it more control over the channel, so it can switch off cleanly at a lower voltage.
  • Wider fins or wider sheets carry more current in the same footprint. Nanosheets let designers choose the sheet width per cell, which fins, fixed in width, could not.
  • CFET is mostly a floor-space trick: stacking n over p halves the width of a logic cell, and it pairs naturally with backside power delivery, which moves the power wires under the transistors.
